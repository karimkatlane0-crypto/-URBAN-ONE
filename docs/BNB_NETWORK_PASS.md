# URBAN ONE B&B Network Pass — reversible design

Status: product/economic model for controlled launch. Percentages are defaults for sensitivity analysis and must be commercially agreed before activation.

## Core proposition

A traveller pays an annual EUR 1,000 Network Pass. The pass provides up to six standard-night credits across participating B&Bs, subject to inventory and booking rules. Within available credits, the member sees EUR 0 as the accommodation charge at stay checkout. Local taxes, optional extras, deposits, damage charges and services outside the pass are excluded.

This is not an unlimited-stay promise. Peak dates or premium rooms may consume more than one credit. After included credits are exhausted, the platform may offer a separately priced network rate.

For participating B&Bs, joining the network does not sell, transfer or encumber the property. The operator retains ownership and operating control. Participation is intended to be reversible under the partner agreement, with confirmed bookings honoured through their agreed stay dates.

## Baseline unit economics

Annual pass gross price: EUR 1,000.

- 72% / EUR 720: host settlement pool.
- 18% / EUR 180: platform gross allocation before fixed costs.
- 5% / EUR 50: risk/refund reserve.
- 5% / EUR 50: payment and operating allowance.

Reference standard-night settlement: EUR 120. That supports a maximum of six standard-night credits from the host settlement pool.

At expected utilization of 4.2 redeemed nights, host settlement is EUR 504 and contribution before fixed platform costs is EUR 396, or 39.6% of gross pass revenue. At full six-night utilization, host settlement is EUR 720 and contribution before fixed costs is EUR 180, or 18.0%.

These figures are not a forecast. They are a control model. Actual economics depend on occupancy, seasonality, cancellation rates, average host settlement, acquisition cost, customer support, fraud/chargebacks, tax treatment and the mix of one-credit versus premium-credit nights.

## Why this design is de-risked

1. **No property sale.** Participation is a distribution/settlement relationship, not an ownership transfer.
2. **Reversible participation.** Partner exit can be contractually available with notice while protecting confirmed bookings.
3. **Capped liability.** The member benefit is credit-bounded rather than unlimited.
4. **Inventory control.** B&Bs can expose eligible rooms/dates rather than surrendering all inventory.
5. **Settlement after eligible stay.** Host payout is linked to a completed qualifying stay, with cancellation/no-show rules defined in the partner agreement.
6. **Negotiable economics.** The 72/18/5/5 allocation is a launch baseline, not a permanent entitlement.
7. **No hidden investment claim.** Market the product as a hospitality membership/network service, not as an investment, guaranteed return, property exit or financial instrument.

## Recommended partner mechanics

- Partner publishes eligible inventory and blackout/peak rules within a policy envelope.
- Standard stay consumes 1 credit; premium/peak stay may consume 1.5–2 credits.
- Settlement amount is fixed or banded by market/season before booking confirmation.
- Host receives settlement after check-in or after a defined fraud/no-show window.
- Member cancellation rules return or partially return credits according to notice period.
- Partner may leave the network with a defined notice period; already confirmed reservations remain enforceable under the agreement.

## Member wording

Preferred: **“EUR 1,000 annual Network Pass. Up to 6 standard-night credits. EUR 0 accommodation charge at eligible checkout while credits remain, subject to availability and booking rules.”**

Avoid: **“unlimited free nights”**, **“free forever”**, **“guaranteed availability”**, or wording that implies every B&B/date is always available.

## Apple payment positioning

The pass funds accommodation consumed outside the app. App Store Review Guideline 3.1.3(e) treats physical goods and services consumed outside the app differently from digital in-app content. Payment implementation must still match the exact service sold, storefront requirements, applicable consumer law, tax rules, payment-provider terms and App Review notes.

## Launch gates

- Partner agreement and settlement schedule approved.
- Consumer terms, cancellation/refund rules and availability language approved.
- Tax/VAT and voucher treatment reviewed for the launch jurisdictions.
- Stripe/Apple Pay flow maps exactly to the physical accommodation service.
- No unlimited-liability marketing claims.
- App Review notes explain the business model and external services.
- Pilot metrics collected before broad rollout: pass conversion, redeemed nights/member, host settlement/night, occupancy lift, cancellation rate, support cost, chargeback rate and contribution margin.
